[0] Retrospective Development of TECR-Net for Ranking Next-Year Recorded Incidents of Non-Compliance on U.S. Offshore Platforms
[1] Abstract
[2] A risk-based offshore regulation regime may benefit from research on ranking recorded administrative outcomes, provided that enforcement records are not treated as direct measures of platform condition or latent violation. This retrospective development study examines TECR-Net, a multi-branch temporal neural network for ranking whether a U.S. offshore platform-year is followed by a recorded Bureau of Safety and Environmental Enforcement Incident of Noncompliance (INC). The archived production-linked study uses exact three-year histories with 26 dynamic, 3 static numerical, and 8 categorical predictors. Because several static and administrative predictors originate from later snapshots or lack source-specific publication dates, the analysis measures retrospective association and does not establish historically deployable forecasting. The reused 2020-2021 development cohort contained 1,884 histories from 982 platforms, including 67 positive outcomes (3.56%). Across seven archived training seeds, mean development AP was 0.07401 +/- 0.00775 and mean development ROC-AUC was 0.63115 +/- 0.01119. A probability-mapped archived ensemble produced apparent development AP 0.07518 and ROC-AUC 0.63855. Under a separate symmetric development benchmark on the same 2020-2021 rows, TECR-Net produced AP 0.05897 and ROC-AUC 0.64408; these values come from a different procedure and do not demonstrate superiority or independent validation. A corrected cohort reconstruction produced 22,957 calendar-exact histories after removing the future-year production-continuity requirement. Equivalent corrected-cohort model comparisons, historically verified predictor timing, complete negative-outcome ascertainment, independently assessed calibration, and an untouched mature-outcome test cohort remain unavailable. Accordingly, the study documents a cohort-reconstruction and retrospective model-development exercise rather than a validated predictive or operational system.
[3] Keywords: offshore platforms; recorded incident of non-compliance; temporal modelling; cross-modal learning; rare-event prediction; inspection prioritization; development study
[4] 1. Introduction
[5] In the oil and gas industry, there are many technical, safety, environmental, and operational standards that must be applied to manage the risks associated with such complicated infrastructure. It is quite difficult to utilize the traditional method of supervision by means of periodic inspection and static checklist due to the heterogeneous standards and huge data involved in the operations. Liu et al. [1] suggested an LLM-assessment framework including semantic indicator extraction, integration weighting, and cloud-model assessment for international pipeline standards. Hui et al. [2] also revealed how the knowledge graph built from over 3,000 pipeline standards could enhance the semantic organization and intelligent retrieval of technical standards. However, both of the research works are about standard creation and knowledge retrieval but not the compliance of the facilities.
[6] A further critical aspect of standardization evaluation that is essential to its effectiveness involves reliable data and sound performance measures. Xia et al. [3] proposed multidimensional control rules for the evaluation of normative compliance, integrity, consistency, accuracy, and timeliness of oil-and-gas surface engineering data using a combination of Analytic Hierarchy Process and entropy weighting. Khordoustan et al. [4] proposed fuzzy Analytic Network Process with machine learning feature importance for the evaluation of contractors’ health, safety, environment, and quality performance. The two studies highlight the utility of heterogeneous evidence in enabling systematic supervision. In this specific context of public BSEE, however, a review of relevant literature does not provide us with a comparable study using platform-year data to jointly model temporally ordered production exposure, operator inspection activity, accidents, platform attributes, and next-year INCs. This is a specific limitation of data and task; it does not suggest that there are no oil-and-gas industry studies combining heterogeneous data sources.
[7] Risk-based asset integrity management provides context for the study. Wahono et al. [5] describe a broader shift from qualitative and deterministic integrity evaluation toward dynamic and probabilistic methods using machine learning. The narrower question examined here is how publicly available BSEE platform histories behave as retrospectively assembled indicators of subsequent recorded INC outcomes. The study does not establish real-time forecasting, identify latent violations, infer causality, or support regulatory decisions.
[8] 1.1 Aim
[9] The goal of this study is to construct and critically assess a temporally ordered retrospective framework in which BSEE INC outcomes recorded in calendar year t+1 are ranked using administrative records associated with platform years t-2, t-1, and t. Because publication and effective dates are unavailable for several attributes, the study evaluates retrospective association and ranking rather than validated historical forecasting.
[10] 1.2 Objectives
[11] Integrate public BSEE production, structure, inspection, enforcement and accident records into auditable platform-year histories
[12] Develop TECR-Net using temporal, static-numerical and categorical features to analyse subsequent recorded INC outcomes
[13] Characterize apparent development-cohort discrimination, probability behavior, baseline comparisons, and component-removal sensitivity without treating them as independent validation
[14] Assess data provenance, temporal consistency and methodological limitations to establish a transparent and reproducible retrospective evaluation framework.
[15] 1.3 Novelty
[16] The methodology novelty comes from the definition of a BSEE platform-year administrative outcome task as precisely ordered calendar history with a provenance-limited archive of dynamically structured, static numerical, and categorical branches, followed by attention-based pooling and residual fusion of learned queries. This novelty is task and implementation specific and does not claim the novelty of temporal networks, attention mechanisms, heterogeneous tabular branches, or multimodal learning within the oil and gas industry.
[17] Contributions
[18] A temporally structured framework for integrating BSEE production, inspection, enforcement and accident records to analyse subsequent recorded non-compliance outcomes
[19] A development-stage assessment of TECR-Net using exact three-year platform histories, seven-seed training, and comparisons performed on an adaptively reused development cohort
[20] A corrected cohort reconstruction that removes the future-production-continuity requirement and corrects identified INC date-parsing inconsistencies, while leaving historical eligibility and predictor-timing questions unresolved
[21] A documented retrospective evaluation framework incorporating model ablation, apparent calibration diagnostics, temporal-integrity checks, and explicit limitations

[29] 2. Literature Survey
[30] Zhang et al. [6] present an overview of risk assessment methods for oil and gas operations, which includes conventional qualitative, quantitative, probabilistic, and intelligent methods. The authors pay special attention to the high dependence of conventional techniques on experts' opinions, as well as the inability of traditional techniques to consider dynamic interaction between risks associated with operation, environment, and equipment. Wahono et al. [7] attempted to overcome this problem by developing intuitionistic fuzzy deep neural network for the purpose of risk assessment in pipelines. It utilizes the fuzzy technique to handle uncertainty and the nonlinear learning capabilities of deep neural networks. Thus, it is evident that these studies show the movement from the traditional risk estimation method to intelligent ones capable of dealing with uncertainty.
[31] Zhou et al. [8] introduced a data-driven approach based on PCA (Principal Component Analysis) and hierarchical clustering to perform an adaptive assessment of gas pipeline networks. PCA decreased the redundancy of indicators, whereas hierarchical clustering allowed grouping of pipelines at risk through the absence of full reliance on pre-set classification. Ye et al. [9] used text mining and Apriori algorithm to classify risks of oil and natural gas pipeline through analysis of historic risk records. Their approach helped reveal common patterns between risk factors and establish a data-driven structure of risk classification. Both examples clearly show benefits of revealing patterns of risks using heterogeneous data. However, clustering and association rule approaches mostly describe existing structures and relations.
[32] AlAbdouli and Al-Shihabi [10] investigated the effects, issues, and evaluation techniques related to the use of AI on the performance in the oil and gas sector. The authors noted the opportunities provided by AI for enhancing predictions, operations, maintenance, and decision making while outlining the issues related to data quality, integration, explainability, and organization readiness. Savari et al. [11] conducted a systematic review of the state of the art machine learning developments in the areas of pipeline reliability-based design, integrity assessment, inspection, and maintenance. According to the authors’ findings, machine learning became an increasingly important tool in assessing condition, predicting failures, planning inspections and optimizing maintenance activities. These papers demonstrate the broader scope of application of AI for industrial performance and pipeline integrity.
[33] Bastek et al. [12] reviewed future directions for data-driven pipeline integrity management, with particular attention to risk assessment, in-line inspection, and machine-learning applications. They emphasized the need to integrate inspection information, physical degradation indicators, and operational data to support proactive integrity decisions. Jones et al. [13] examined artificial-intelligence applications for offshore oil and gas pipeline asset integrity management. Their study discussed how intelligent methods can enhance corrosion monitoring, defect assessment, predictive maintenance, and failure prevention throughout the asset lifecycle. Collectively, these studies support the transition from reactive maintenance toward predictive and risk-based integrity management. Nevertheless, existing research generally prioritizes physical asset condition and failure probability, while regulatory compliance, inspection history, and multidimensional standardization performance remain comparatively underexplored.
[34] Al-Ammari et al. [14] reviewed and demonstrated digital-twin applications for gas-pipeline leak detection and fault diagnosis. Their work showed how virtual representations, sensor information, and analytical models can support continuous monitoring and timely identification of abnormal pipeline conditions. Cai and Wang [15] subsequently proposed a digital-twin-driven leakage-detection method for natural gas pipelines, further illustrating the potential of synchronized physical and virtual systems for real-time supervision. Both studies advance dynamic pipeline monitoring by linking operational data with intelligent diagnostic models. However, digital-twin systems often require dense sensor networks, detailed physical models, and continuous data streams. Such requirements limit their direct applicability to regulatory datasets, where observations are commonly periodic, heterogeneous, and recorded at facility or operator level rather than as continuous sensor measurements.
[35] Ali et al. [16] reviewed artificial-intelligence and machine-learning methods for predicting and optimizing production in oil and gas surface networks. Their study demonstrated the ability of data-driven models to capture nonlinear relationships among operational variables and support production planning and network optimization. Chen et al. [17] extended production monitoring through a progression from clustering to federated learning for unconventional natural gas wells. Their collaborative approach enables multiple data sources or operational units to contribute to model development while reducing centralized data-sharing requirements. These studies demonstrate the importance of scalable learning from distributed and heterogeneous industrial data. However, production optimization alone does not represent standardization performance. A comprehensive supervision framework must connect operational behaviour with facility characteristics, inspection activities, incidents, and regulatory outcomes.
[36] Lopes et al. [18] developed an open-world learning approach for detecting and classifying anomalies in oil-well production. Unlike closed-set classifiers, open-world learning can identify observations that do not belong to previously known event classes, making it relevant for evolving industrial environments. Aranha et al. [19] applied a Transformer-based method to anomaly detection in intelligent well completions, using the architecture’s ability to learn complex temporal dependencies within multivariate operational sequences. Both studies demonstrate that advanced learning methods can identify abnormal behaviour that conventional fixed-class models may overlook. Nevertheless, operational anomalies do not automatically represent regulatory non-compliance or inadequate standardization. Their practical use in supervision therefore requires integration with inspection records, compliance histories, interpretable risk indicators, and decision rules that translate anomalies into actionable regulatory or managerial priorities.
[37] Verma et al. [20] proposed DeepPipeNet for deep-learning-based anomaly detection in oil and gas pipeline monitoring, illustrating the growing role of specialized neural architectures in identifying complex pipeline abnormalities. Its emphasis on automated monitoring complements the open-world anomaly framework of Lopes et al. [18], which addresses previously unseen operational conditions. While DeepPipeNet strengthens pattern-recognition capability, both approaches remain centred on detecting abnormal physical behaviour rather than evaluating broader compliance performance. This distinction is important because standardization supervision must account for operational exposure, structural characteristics, inspection frequency, previous violations, enforcement actions, and safety incidents. Consequently, anomaly-detection outputs should be treated as one component of a larger explainable evaluation framework rather than as a complete measure of facility standardization or regulatory risk.
[38] The endpoint is also a part of the administrative decision data and selectively labeled data literature. As BSEE defines the INC as an official enforcement notice that results from an inspector identifying a regulatory violation and running a risk-based inspection program using facility-specific INC, incident, and production data, the attention of the inspection is drawn based on this process. Thus, selective labeling of observations depends on the manner in which inspections are conducted. In general terms, selective observation of administrative decisions allows the prediction of the decision process and not the underlying state itself.

[42] 3. Proposed Methodology
[43] 3.1 Prediction Target
[44] For platform i and feature year t, the binary label equals 1 when the available data contain at least one recorded INC in calendar year t+1. No explicit t+1 outcome field is included in the predictor whitelist. The estimand is therefore the ranking of future recorded INC outcomes, not latent non-compliance, accident occurrence, or platform safety. Inspection frequency, reporting, enforcement, appeals, and delayed recording may affect whether an INC is observed. Platform-level labels use the calendar year of the INC date, and operator-level aggregates use the available INC date field; subsequent adjustments were not traced. The intended origin is the end of year t, but release-specific publication lags and effective dates are unavailable for several predictors. Consequently, the analysis does not establish that every predictor was available at a historical prediction origin. TECR-Net is evaluated only as a retrospective research-ranking model and not as a safety determination, enforcement decision, production-authorization tool, or real-time system.

[51] 3.2 Data Integration
[52] Archival files on public BSEE releases for platform structures, platform-level production, platform INCs, operator inspections and INCs, accident investigations, and companies and lease ownership were merged at the platform-year level. The date of download from the original archives was not recorded; therefore, exact reconstruction of the original data vintage is impossible and is mentioned as a limitation. The release comparison audit performed on 17 October 2026 preserved the file hashes and found ongoing changes, with 127 new or changed rows for platform-INC in 2026 showing publication delay. The number of unique platform-years in the archived full panel was 148,672, and in the archived model panel, it was 141,730, with a difference of 6,942 rows. In the original design based on production linkage, there were 13,416 histories in the full panel for which the t−2 to t histories were exact and a t+1 observation row existed, while in the frozen panel, their number was 12,280; however, in all the missing histories, the t+1 row was missing in the model panel. Production was aggregated into platform years and merged to the structure, platform INCs, operator year inspection and enforcement, and lease year accident data. The archived panel is the foundation of the archived results; the corrected-cohort analysis in the corrected reconstruction analysis is a distinct reconstruction and its statistics should not be blended into the same results. The archived model assumes feature year 2014 due to the fact that it is the earliest year available in the frozen analysis pipeline which has at least three years of history as well as overlapping predictor generation. Year-specific source-specific completeness was not maintained and hence 2014 is a limitation of analysis and not a verified starting point for completeness in BSEE coverage. Production data in the archived builder is aggregated using normalized platform key and calendar year while operator, lease, area and block are from the last production data record in the year. Structure data is joined using normalized platform key after resolving duplicates, platform INCs using platform key and INC issue year, operator inspection/enforcement aggregates using operator code and year, and accident proxies using lease and year.Complete unmatched-row counts were not retained. Historical operator responsibility therefore represents the operator recorded in the final monthly production row, not a separately verified dated ownership assignment.

[59] Description of the original archival production-linked experiment is provided in Table 3. The eligibility criteria in this experiment stipulated feature years t-2, t-1, and t and a visible production-linked t+1 row, conditioning participation in the experiment on future activity. A subsequent reconciliation showed 1,136 histories that were otherwise eligible were excluded from the experiment at the t+1 model-panel step. This corrects for the main production-continuation problem, although a full P2 pass is not guaranteed since installation and removal dates came from a more recent snapshot, eligibility according to regulations at each issuance date was not verified, and lack of an INC was not verified to be a negative outcome. The operational forecast convention thus consists of research information available at a documented cutoff after year t predicting an observed INC issuance in t+1; since source-specific publication lags are unknown, all analyses are retrospective.
[60] 3.4 Leakage Controls, Feature Provenance, and Preprocessing
[61] Preprocessing of numerical variables was computed solely on the basis of the fitting period. Outliers beyond the 1st and 99th percentiles of the fitting period were truncated, missing values were imputed using medians for the fitting period, and the resulting variables were robustly standardized using the medians and interquartile ranges of the fitting period. The same parameters saved during this preprocessing step were used without modification on the development set. The categories were also trained on the fitting set, where missing and new categories were labeled unknown.
[62] Installation, removal, datum-year and site-clearance variables have been detected from the source schema audit of the platform-structure dataset released. However, effective dates for individual attributes have not been detected. Installation dates could potentially be used to conduct platform existence validation; however, they do not validate the years when values for water depth, structure type, region, heliport, personnel attendance and manning became valid or available. The full-feature model does not make any historical availability claims on these attributes. No region code source attribute was identified as such; it is treated as derived. Operator inspection and enforcement counts are indexed by year, but they are not normalized by exposure of fleet and the historically responsible operator has not been independently verified. Accident values from lease-year periods may be shared among more than one platform; platforms-per-lease and unmatched event distribution has not been retained. These data are reported as retrospective contextual associations. A time valid model excluding unverifiable predictors has not been run yet.

[68] 3.5 TECR-Net Architecture
[69] TECR-Net operationally separates predictors into three feature branches: dynamic platform-year history, static numerical attributes, and categorical platform context. These are feature groups, not sensor modalities such as image, text, or audio. Each branch has a separate encoder and a common latent width before learned-query pooling. The present experiments do not establish that this grouping is better than simple concatenation; the branch structure is therefore treated as an investigated design rather than a validated architectural advantage.
[70] 3.5.1 Feature Gate and Bidirectional Temporal Encoder
[71] The learnable global feature-gating component provides a rescaling factor for each of the 26 dynamic inputs (set initially to 0.5); as the subsequent weights can correct for rescaling, they do not constitute identifiable features for predictive importance. The global gates are the same for all the platforms and time points; thus, the network is able to learn consistent rescaling of features. The gated features then get passed to a single-layer bidirectional gated recurrent unit (BiGRU) layer with 80 units each way, to account for temporal relationships of the three-year-long data. The GRU component utilizes the recurrent gated formulation.

[84] 3.5.3 Static and Categorical Encoders
[85] Static encoder encodes three numerical attributes of the platform, namely water depth, latitude, and longitude, by applying the transformation through two feed-forward layers with GELU activation, followed by dropout and layer normalization. These embeddings are concatenated and projected to the latent space with dimensionality 80.

[91] 3.5.4 Cross-Modal Attention and Residual Fusion
[92] The temporal-static-numerical and categorical tokens are combined using an eight-head learned query attention-pooling mechanism. A trainable query computes a weighted sum of the three independently encoded tokens; this is not token swapping. An arithmetic mean provides a residual pathway, retaining the information present in all branches. The concatenated pool and mean result in a 160-dimensional representation, which is inputted to a two-layer classification head with GELU activation and dropout layers, to yield a logit, the sigmoid of which is the model prediction for a recorded INC at t+1. [99] 3.6 Optimization and Development Procedure
[100] Alpha-balanced focal loss was applied to cope with class imbalance. For the logit z, probability p, and label y, p_t is equal to p if y = 1 and 1 − p if not, whereas the loss function is the batch-wise mean of α_t(1 − p_t)^γ multiplied by the binary cross-entropy, where α_t equals α for positives and 1 − α for negatives. The coefficient α is the ratio of negative labels in the training sequences of the fitting period (approximately 0.934 for 538 positives out of 8,200 sequences), constrained to the range from 0.55 to 0.95, and γ is the hyperparameter. The implementation leverages a gated bidirectional GRU, additive temporal attention, multi-head attention, layer normalization, AdamW optimizer, Optuna [39,40] with TPE sampler, and scikit-learn. TECR-Net hyperparameters and early stopping strategy were chosen using the 2020–2021 development cohort. For each random seed, a logistic regression model (L-BFGS solver with default L2 regularization and C = 1) implemented in scikit-learn was fitted to the output sigmoid probabilities of the 2020–2021 sequences using the Platt method but probability rather than logit inputs, after which the seven transformed values were averaged. The logits were cut off at ±30 before the sigmoid function. The probability-to-probability mapping for this transformation is referred to as Platt-like mapping as opposed to the standard logit input Platt scaling. The threshold for maximum F1 score was also chosen on the basis of the development set instead of the separate ensembles’ scores (or alternatively a quantile of 2,000 for those in excess). The ensemble threshold (0.04909, Table 8) is thus not equal to the average threshold for each of the seeds (0.04982, Table 7). The F1-maximizing threshold is a statistical convention and does not contain any considerations for the cost, benefits or budget for inspection. The original replacement tabular baseline had an inner holdout of 2018-2019 with 25 runs per model. A symmetric rerun of this process followed using the identical selection period (2018-2019), 25 trial budget and seven-seed sequence for TECR-Net, plain GRU, CatBoost, LightGBM, XGBoost, HistGradientBoosting and logistic regression. The untouched requirement has yet to be satisfied.

[103] The implementation configuration necessary to replicate the experiment from its archive is provided in Table 5. Explicitly specified are AdamW optimizer, adaptive learning rate decay, gradient clipping, focal loss parameters, Optuna search, and early stopping. The architecture is fixed for the seven final seeds, while initialization and mini-batch ordering are not; seed variation corresponds to stochastic training alone and not uncertainty in generalizing to a new hardware platform, year, or calibration. For Optuna search space, hidden size was {48, 64, 80, 96, 128}, GRU layers were {1, 2}, attention heads {2, 4, 8} of the hidden size, dropout was 0.12-0.38, learning rate 2×10−4 to 3×10−3 (logarithmic scale), weight decay 10−6 to 10−3 (logarithmic scale), γ was 1.25-3.0, and batch size was {128, 256, 512}. Every trial consisted of up to 75 epochs with patience 15. The chosen configuration had one GRU layer, hidden size of 80, and 8 heads. Implementation choices left out of reporting include sigmoid initialization of gates at 0.5, a learned query initialized with standard deviation 0.02, attention dropout being equal to model dropout, and categorical embeddings sizes min(16, max(3, √cardinality)). Excluding the categorical embeddings and encoder, the model has at least 123,788 trainable parameters. Because TECR-Net selection, probability mapping, threshold determination, and reporting used the same development period, the resulting estimates may be optimistic for unseen data.
[104] 3.7 Evaluation Measures
[105] Average precision (AP), computed by sklearn.metrics.average_precision_score, is the main measure without any threshold since positives are scarce. Formerly archived figures and files had been labeled with a vague precision-recall name for this measure; the updated paper refers to AP because average precision is not equivalent to the trapezoid area under the precision-recall curve. When working with very imbalanced binary responses, the precision-recall approach focuses on positive gains and is more revealing than the ROC approach. ROC-AUC reflects ranking through all possible thresholds. F1, F2, precision, recall, specificity, balanced accuracy, MCC, and the confusion matrix rely on the threshold set during development. Brier score reflects squared probability error, while Brier skill is one minus model Brier score over constant-reference Brier score. The constant reference is the development prevalence. Precision and lift at k = 50, 100 and 200 refer to ranked platform-year pairs rather than guaranteed distinct platforms. Probability and calibration measures are reported only as apparent development diagnostics.

[107] 
[108] Table 6 contrasts ranking metrics with threshold-dependent metrics. It is important to understand that the threshold used was chosen on development data. AP and ROC-AUC are order-based metrics, while F1 and the confusion matrix are order-based metrics that also consider the choice of operating point. Platform-clustered bootstrap confidence intervals are calculated on predictions based on paired bootstrap sampling of platforms. The provided manuscript does not include a complete trace of the number of replicates, seed, treatment of zero-positive replicates, and higher-level operator/year/bootstraping. As such, the confidence intervals are considered descriptive archived outputs rather than independently reproducible confirmation.

[122] 4. Results
[123] The 2020–2021 cohort supported hyperparameter selection, early stopping, probability mapping, threshold selection and performance reporting. Consequently, all results from this cohort are apparent development estimates rather than independent validation estimates. Confidence intervals reported in this section are development-cohort bootstrap intervals; they describe sampling uncertainty within the development cohort, not generalization.
[124] 4.1 Multi-Seed Stability
[125] Table 7. Apparent development-cohort seed-level performance across seven TECR-Net fits
[126] The mean AP of the development data using TECR-Net was 0.07401 +/- 0.00775, and ROC-AUC was 0.63115 +/- 0.01119 over seven random seeds. Recall was highly varied over the seven seeds (SD 0.14861), suggesting that the behavior of the algorithm is sensitive to initialization and training variability. These are the metrics of stochasticity present in the adaptive development set; they are not confidence intervals, and therefore not indicative of independent generalization.

[155] Table 10 reports the full model and six component-removal variants using development results only.
[156] Table 10. Development-cohort architectural ablation using saved ensemble outputs
[157] 
[158] The removal of GRU resulted in the largest change in AP, decreasing from 0.07518 to 0.05556. [166] 4.7 Development Uncertainty and Apparent Calibration
[167] Table 12 reports dependence-aware uncertainty and limited probability diagnostics calculated from the development ensemble predictions. Because the probability mapping was fitted on the same period, these quantities are apparent rather than independently validated calibration estimates.
[168] Table 12. Platform-clustered development uncertainty and apparent calibration diagnostics
[169] 
[170] The archived probability mapping pipeline was compared to the saved pipeline: logits are truncated to ±30, transformed to probabilities through the sigmoid function, independently mapped for each seed through a regularized logistic regression (L-BFGS, C = 1, intercept included) with the raw probability as the sole input, and averaged across the seven probability maps. The intercept of 2.57119 and slope of 1.79530 obtained here are descriptive estimates from the development set and deviate significantly from the ideal intercept and slope of 0 and 1 respectively. This implementation check clears up the order of transformations but lacks a separate calibration check. The low Brier score is at least partially attributable to low prevalence, while the reference prediction employs retrospective prevalence figures.
[171] The apparent calibration diagnostics are unfavorable: the intercept and slope differ from their ideal values, and the model Brier score of 0.03406 is only about 0.7% lower than the constant-prevalence reference of 0.03430. Because mapping and assessment used the same development cohort, the scores must not be interpreted as calibrated absolute probabilities. AP and ROC-AUC describe ranking on the development data, whereas the Brier score, reliability diagram, intercept, and slope are apparent development characteristics only.

[181] 5. Discussion
[182] The archived probability-mapped ensemble produced apparent development AP 0.07518 and ROC-AUC 0.63855 on the adaptively reused 2020-2021 cohort. At its selected threshold, 54 of 67 positive outcomes (80.6%) were missed, 134 of 147 predicted-positive sequences (91.2%) were negative, and 4 of the 50 highest-ranked sequences were positive. These results do not establish an efficient inspection-allocation rule. In the separate symmetric development benchmark, TECR-Net had AP 0.05897, a descriptive difference of 0.00744 from CatBoost. Because the cohort was reused and no paired confirmation was performed on untouched corrected data, this difference does not establish superiority. Component-removal results likewise show sensitivity rather than architectural necessity: removing the GRU produced the largest AP decrease, whereas removing learned-query attention pooling changed AP by only 0.00024.
[183] The interpretation is deliberately narrow. A recorded INC is an administrative enforcement outcome observed through inspection, reporting, and adjudication processes; it is not a direct measure of latent violation, safety, or fitness for production. The corrected reconstruction removes the future-year production-continuity requirement and corrects identified date-parsing problems, but it does not establish historical as-of-date validity, complete negative-outcome ascertainment, comparative model performance, independent calibration, or prospective usefulness.
[184] 
[185] 6. Limitations
[186] Cohort Construction, Data Provenance, and Historical Eligibility: Even though the cohort construction increased the consistency of the data, resolved date parsing issues with INC dates, and dropped the necessity of production continuity the following year, there are still some uncertainties in terms of methodology. The original criteria were no longer available to be reconstructed, and historical eligibility for the regulation has not been confirmed independently for each platform year. Also, many of the structural and administrative variables come from subsequent data releases, whose exact dates and time of release are unknown.
[187] Model Comparison and Architectural Assessment: Seven seeds characterize stochastic training variation, not uncertainty across independent cohorts. Symmetric model comparisons and component removals were performed on the archived development data and do not establish predictive superiority or architectural necessity. Equivalent corrected-cohort comparisons, a tuned simple-concatenation model, fairly tuned ablations, and confirmation on an untouched cohort remain absent. Dependence among platform, operator, and lease records further limits conventional uncertainty estimates.
[188] Evaluation and Outcome Validity: Independent evaluation is not possible due to the use of the development cohorts to select the model and the reevaluation of the historical cohorts. In addition, the target for prediction is Incidents of Non-Compliance that have been documented as opposed to the actual regulatory violation, and missing inspections and reporting information can impact the labeling process. An evaluation methodology has been developed for the future, although there has been no independent evaluation of an untapped cohort with adequate maturity of the outcome.
[189] Probability Calibration, Interface Consistency, and Reproducibility: Probability mapping and the operating threshold were fitted using development observations, so calibration and threshold performance have not been independently assessed. The interface screenshots are illustrative and were not independently validated against a frozen corrected model package. Corrected-model checkpoints, preprocessing objects, calibrator, and threshold artifacts were not preserved and must be regenerated before a complete reproducibility package can be released.
[190] 
[191] 7. Conclusion
[192] This study documents TECR-Net as a retrospective method for ranking next-year recorded BSEE INC outcomes from exact three-year platform histories. The archived probability-mapped ensemble produced apparent development AP 0.07518 and ROC-AUC 0.63855; a separate symmetric eight-model development benchmark produced TECR-Net AP 0.05897 and ROC-AUC 0.64408. These values arise from different procedures on adaptively reused data and must not be combined or treated as independent validation. The corrected cohort reconstruction removes the future-year production-continuity requirement and corrects identified mixed-format INC dates, but it has not yet received equivalent model comparisons. Historical predictor availability, negative-outcome completeness, independent calibration, and an untouched mature-outcome evaluation remain unresolved. The defensible contribution is therefore the documented cohort reconstruction and retrospective development analysis, not a validated forecasting system or operational inspection tool.
[193] Availability of Data and Code and Declarations. The data used in this study originate from public BSEE records. Archived materials include sequence indexes, model predictions, preprocessing parameters, weights, and internal replay reports for the frozen archived pipeline. These materials do not establish independent validity. Corrected-model weights and the associated preprocessing, calibration, and threshold artifacts were not preserved and must be regenerated and verified before release as a reproducibility package. Repository location, source-code version, environment and hardware details, licensing, funding, conflicts of interest, author contributions, affiliations, and other journal declarations must be completed by the authors. The computational study uses no human participants or personal information.
