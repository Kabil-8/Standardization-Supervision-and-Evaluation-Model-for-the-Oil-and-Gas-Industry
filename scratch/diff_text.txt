 Retrospective Development of TECR-Net for Ranking Next-Year Recorded Incidents of Non-Compliance on U.S. Offshore Platforms
 Abstract
-A risk-based offshore regulation regime could potentially profit from employing methods of ranking recorded administrative results while maintaining the separation between the record of enforcement and the state of the platform itself. This retrospective development study examines TECR-Net, a multi-branch temporal neural network designed to estimate whether a U.S. offshore platform-year leads to a recorded Bureau of Safety and Environmental Enforcement Incident of Noncompliance (INC). The archived production-based study uses exactly three-year histories with 26 dynamic, 3 static numerical and 8 categorical predictors. Several static and administrative predictors come from later records or do not have source-specific dates of publication; thus, the full-feature analysis is a case of retrospective association modelling without the proof that all inputs were known at the time of a historical forecast origin. The 2020-2021 development cohort consisted of 1,884 histories from 982 platforms, of which 67 resulted in positive outcomes (3.56%). In seven separately trained archived seeds, average AP was 0.07401 ± 0.00775, average ROC-AUC was 0.63115 ± 0.01119. The probability mapped ensemble with archival training developed AP 0.07518 and ROC-AUC 0.63855 through adaptive development. An independent run with an eight-model symmetric design, utilizing the same selection of 2018 and 2019 years, identical rows in 2020 and 2021 years and protocol gave TECR-Net the results of AP 0.05897 and ROC-AUC 0.64408. Such comparison stays developmental and does not prove any external superiority. The corrected-cohort study has interrupted the production continuity in t+1 year and produced 22,957 records with calendar exact histories, but the data on predictor time validity, completeness of ascertainment of negative outcomes, corrected data comparator reruns and intact mature-outcome cohort are not available.

 1. Introduction
 In the oil and gas industry, there are many technical, safety, environmental, and operational standards that must be applied to manage the risks associated with such complicated infrastructure. It is quite difficult to utilize the traditional method of supervision by means of periodic inspection and static checklist due to the heterogeneous standards and huge data involved in the operations. Liu et al. [1] suggested an LLM-assessment framework including semantic indicator extraction, integration weighting, and cloud-model assessment for international pipeline standards. Hui et al. [2] also revealed how the knowledge graph built from over 3,000 pipeline standards could enhance the semantic organization and intelligent retrieval of technical standards. However, both of the research works are about standard creation and knowledge retrieval but not the compliance of the facilities.
 A further critical aspect of standardization evaluation that is essential to its effectiveness involves reliable data and sound performance measures. Xia et al. [3] proposed multidimensional control rules for the evaluation of normative compliance, integrity, consistency, accuracy, and timeliness of oil-and-gas surface engineering data using a combination of Analytic Hierarchy Process and entropy weighting. Khordoustan et al. [4] proposed fuzzy Analytic Network Process with machine learning feature importance for the evaluation of contractors’ health, safety, environment, and quality performance. The two studies highlight the utility of heterogeneous evidence in enabling systematic supervision. In this specific context of public BSEE, however, a review of relevant literature does not provide us with a comparable study using platform-year data to jointly model temporally ordered production exposure, operator inspection activity, accidents, platform attributes, and next-year INCs. This is a specific limitation of data and task; it does not suggest that there are no oil-and-gas industry studies combining heterogeneous data sources.

 3.6 Optimization and Development Procedure
 Alpha-balanced focal loss was applied to cope with class imbalance. For the logit z, probability p, and label y, p_t is equal to p if y = 1 and 1 − p if not, whereas the loss function is the batch-wise mean of α_t(1 − p_t)^γ multiplied by the binary cross-entropy, where α_t equals α for positives and 1 − α for negatives. The coefficient α is the ratio of negative labels in the training sequences of the fitting period (approximately 0.934 for 538 positives out of 8,200 sequences), constrained to the range from 0.55 to 0.95, and γ is the hyperparameter. The implementation leverages a gated bidirectional GRU, additive temporal attention, multi-head attention, layer normalization, AdamW optimizer, Optuna [39,40] with TPE sampler, and scikit-learn. TECR-Net hyperparameters and early stopping strategy were chosen using the 2020–2021 development cohort. For each random seed, a logistic regression model (L-BFGS solver with default L2 regularization and C = 1) implemented in scikit-learn was fitted to the output sigmoid probabilities of the 2020–2021 sequences using the Platt method but probability rather than logit inputs, after which the seven transformed values were averaged. The logits were cut off at ±30 before the sigmoid function. The probability-to-probability mapping for this transformation is referred to as Platt-like mapping as opposed to the standard logit input Platt scaling. The threshold for maximum F1 score was also chosen on the basis of the development set instead of the separate ensembles’ scores (or alternatively a quantile of 2,000 for those in excess). The ensemble threshold (0.04909, Table 8) is thus not equal to the average threshold for each of the seeds (0.04982, Table 7). The F1-maximizing threshold is a statistical convention and does not contain any considerations for the cost, benefits or budget for inspection. The original replacement tabular baseline had an inner holdout of 2018-2019 with 25 runs per model. A symmetric rerun of this process followed using the identical selection period (2018-2019), 25 trial budget and seven-seed sequence for TECR-Net, plain GRU, CatBoost, LightGBM, XGBoost, HistGradientBoosting and logistic regression. The untouched requirement has yet to be satisfied.

 3.7 Evaluation Measures
 Average precision (AP), computed by sklearn.metrics.average_precision_score, is the main measure without any threshold since positives are scarce. Formerly archived figures and files had been labeled with a vague precision-recall name for this measure; the updated paper refers to AP because average precision is not equivalent to the trapezoid area under the precision-recall curve. When working with very imbalanced binary responses, the precision-recall approach focuses on positive gains and is more revealing than the ROC approach. ROC-AUC reflects ranking through all possible thresholds. F1, F2, precision, recall, specificity, balanced accuracy, MCC, and the confusion matrix rely on the threshold set during development. Brier score reflects squared probability error, while Brier skill is one minus model Brier score over constant-reference Brier score. The constant reference is the development prevalence. Precision and lift at k = 50, 100 and 200 refer to ranked platform-year pairs rather than guaranteed distinct platforms. Probability and calibration measures are reported only as apparent development diagnostics.

 6. Limitations
 Cohort Construction, Data Provenance, and Historical Eligibility: Even though the cohort construction increased the consistency of the data, resolved date parsing issues with INC dates, and dropped the necessity of production continuity the following year, there are still some uncertainties in terms of methodology. The original criteria were no longer available to be reconstructed, and historical eligibility for the regulation has not been confirmed independently for each platform year. Also, many of the structural and administrative variables come from subsequent data releases, whose exact dates and time of release are unknown.
-Comparison Between Models and Architectural Validation: The researchers used seven training seeds to test the performance of TECR-Net through comparisons of symmetric baselines and component removals. But the comparisons have been made mainly on the archived development set and cannot be considered as independent predictors of superiority. Baseline comparisons, equally tuned architectural ablation tests and temporal control experiments are still lacking. Moreover, the lack of role of cross-modal attention and possible dependence among the platform, operator and lease datasets add to these limitations.
+Model Comparison and Architectural Assessment: Seven seeds characterize stochastic training variation, not uncertainty across independent cohorts. Symmetric model comparisons and component removals were performed on the archived development data and do not establish predictive superiority or architectural necessity. Equivalent corrected-cohort comparisons, a tuned simple-concatenation model, fairly tuned ablations, and confirmation on an untouched cohort remain absent. Dependence among platform, operator, and lease records further limits conventional uncertainty estimates.
 Evaluation and Outcome Validity: Independent evaluation is not possible due to the use of the development cohorts to select the model and the reevaluation of the historical cohorts. In addition, the target for prediction is Incidents of Non-Compliance that have been documented as opposed to the actual regulatory violation, and missing inspections and reporting information can impact the labeling process. An evaluation methodology has been developed for the future, although there has been no independent evaluation of an untapped cohort with adequate maturity of the outcome.
-Probability Calibration, Interface Uniformity and Reproducibility : The probability calibration and operating threshold of the original were determined by development observations, hindering any independent assessment of calibration. While the modified analysis process allowed separating probability calibration from threshold selection and had previously shown perfect replaying of predictions inside the system, the artifacts of the corrected model, including all checkpoints, pre-processing objects, calibrator, and threshold object, were not saved. The current screenshots of the interface were not independently tested for the frozen model.
+Probability Calibration, Interface Consistency, and Reproducibility: Probability mapping and the operating threshold were fitted using development observations, so calibration and threshold performance have not been independently assessed.  7. Conclusion
-In this study, TECR-Net was formulated as a retrospective approach to the ranking of next year's BSEE Incident of Non-Compliance recorded events using the exact history of three-year platforms. The probability-mapped ensemble trained on the archive obtained an apparent development AP of 0.07518 and ROC-AUC of 0.63855, while the symmetric eight-model comparison resulted in AP of 0.05897 and ROC-AUC of 0.64408 under a common development protocol. The corrected cohort construction has resolved issues of mixed-format INC dating and eliminated the need for the future-year production continuity, but the problems of predictor availability in the historical data, completeness of negative outcomes, comparative evaluation of corrected data, and independent validation of mature outcomes remain open. Thus, the main contribution here is a transparent and verifiable framework for retrospective modelling of administrative outcomes.
